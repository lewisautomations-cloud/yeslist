When I applied to Goldman Sachs, I messaged 4 Xavier alumni who work there. None answered. When I messaged 4 alumni who already knew me, all 4 did. Students worry about that first silence before they ever hit send. Yohan Lewis, Xavier student and builder of YesList

How a student uses it

  1. Pick your major.Or tap Undecided and pick the areas you're interested in. Or search for a major, a company, a job or a city.
  2. Read the cards.Each alum shows where they work, a short note in their own words, and what they offer.
  3. Tap Request.Pick what you're asking about and write your questions. Asking about an internship? Attach your résumé (PDF or Word).
  4. Tap Send request.You'll see a copy of exactly what the alum sees.

Testing it? Save your résumé to your phone first, in case you ask about an internship.

What alumni offer

Advice

A short call to answer your questions.

Paid project

A short paid task, roughly 20–40 hours, done around classes.

Internship

A summer or semester internship where the alum works.

Each alum offers any mix of the three. Every alum offers at least one.

How it's different

Handshake

Lists jobs. It doesn't show which Xavier alumni will talk to you.

LinkedIn

Shows who went to Xavier, not who is open to hearing from a student.

Xavier's Flash Mentoring Program

Connects students with professionals in any field for a conversation. YesList shows Xavier alumni, sorted by your major, who have already said what they'll give: advice, a paid project or an internship.

BrewChat

Lets students ask professionals and alumni from anywhere for a coffee chat; the person says yes or no after the student asks. On YesList, every alum has said yes to Xavier students before anyone asks.

Ten Thousand Coffees

Some universities offer it to their students and alumni; it introduces them to each other for career conversations. On YesList, each alum says up front what they'll give: advice, a paid project or an internship.

For alumni: raising your hand

In a real version, an alum would sign up and say:

  • which majors they'll help (any majors, not only their own),
  • the kind of work they do,
  • what they can offer: advice, a paid project, an internship, or a mix,
  • one line in their own words about what students can ask them.

Students only see alumni who have done this, so every request goes to someone who already said yes. The alumni side is not built yet; this demo uses sample alumni.

Starting at Xavier

YesList starts with one school. If Xavier students send requests and alumni answer, other schools get their own YesList, and their students see only their own alumni.
